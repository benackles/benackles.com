Most positioning fails because it focuses on what the product is, rather than how the buyer survives. In highly competitive ecosystems, a strong product marketing strategy doesn't list features—it changes the rules of the comparison entirely.

## 1. Identifying True Competitors

The competitor is rarely another venture-backed startup. More often, it is inertia, manual spreadsheets, or a custom-built internal system that barely functions but is already paid for. To win, you must first articulate why the status quo is actively costing your buyer energy and capital.

> "Positioning isn't a copywriting exercise. It's a cross-functional alignment tool. If engineering and product don't agree with your differentiation axis, your sales team never will."

## 2. Defining Your Differentiation Axes

A common trap is trying to be better at everything. True differentiation requires choosing what you are going to focus on. Map your product across critical, high-impact axes where your architectural decisions give you an asymmetric and defensible advantage.

```md title="POSITIONING_STATEMENT_TEMPLATE.md"
# The Strategic Core Formula

For [Target Customer Profile]
Who struggles with [Unmet Pain Point / Current Workarounds]
Our product, [Product Name], is a [Product Category / Market Segment]
That delivers [Unique Primary Differentiating Capability]
Unlike [Status Quo / Primary Alternative]
We enable [Measurable High-Impact GTM Business Outcome]
```

## 3. Continuous Testing with Real Customers

Positioning is a living hypothesis. Test it early in your sales cycles, analyze cold-call feedback, and track messaging resonance. If your buyers aren't repeating your core value hook back to you on call 2, it isn't working.
